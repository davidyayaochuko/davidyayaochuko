If you cannot delete it, you should not create it

16 October 2026 · David Ochuko Yaya

I still teach cloud foundations. Not as a second career. Because explaining a thing is how I find out whether I understand it — and because the people coming through Thrive Africa and MEQuest are often opening a real console for the first time.

The first lab is never “launch something impressive.” It is MFA, a budget with an email alert, and a tour of the pages that charge you money. Then we build. Then we tear it down before the session ends.

The rule

If you cannot delete it, you should not create it. That covers the EC2 instance, the disk you forgot, the public storage bucket, the NAT gateway someone enabled “just to try.” It also covers the production habit: every environment needs an owner, a spend ceiling, and a way back.

I learned this the expensive way, the way most of us do — a resource left running, a root account used like a daily login, a pipeline that could deploy and could not roll back cleanly. At Netan the work became hybrid cloud, Terraform, and a platform that had to stay up. The lesson did not get softer when the title changed to CTO. It got stricter.

Why cohorts hear it out loud

The Dev Guide 99 exists for the same reason the labs do. People do not get unstuck from a PDF. They get unstuck when someone who has already broken production will sit with the diagram and say what to delete first.
