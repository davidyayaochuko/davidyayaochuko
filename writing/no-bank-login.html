Why Spendprofile does not connect to your bank

16 October 2026 · David Ochuko Yaya

Most products that promise to “read your money” start the same way. Connect your bank. Share a credential, or hand an aggregator a token, and hope the person on the other side of the screen trusts you enough to click allow.

We built Spendprofile the other way. You export the PDF your Nigerian bank already gave you. You upload it. We turn the pages into transactions, categories, and a picture of cash flow. Then we check our own work against the statement’s printed totals.

That last step is the product. Anyone can OCR a PDF. A confidently wrong number is worse than an incomplete one. If the extraction does not reconcile, we say so. We do not dress a gap up as insight.

The permission surface

No bank login. No BVN. The only thing we ask for is a file the user already holds — the same file they would email to a consulate, a lender, or a landlord. That is a smaller promise, and it is one we can keep.

It also made the engineering harder. Nigerian banks do not share a format. Fourteen of them parse through dedicated work, not a slogan that we support “any statement.” When a format is unsupported, the honest outcome is unsupported — not a guess.

Why this is a CTO decision

Open banking is a real path. It is also a licence, a sponsor, a recurring cost, and a sentence you must be able to defend in public. We are not there, and we do not pretend we are. The commercial story for businesses that would send us their customers’ statements stays closed until the data-protection registration that role requires is done.

Until then, the consumer product does one job well: help someone read the statement before someone else judges them by it.
